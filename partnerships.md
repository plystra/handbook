# Plystra Partnerships

## Purpose

A Plystra partnership is a way for an excellent independent developer, or a very small studio, to build a product together with Plystra and release it worldwide. Plystra works on the product itself: design, code, writing, and review. Plystra LLC, a United States company, sells the product and collects payment from customers around the world. When the product meets the Plystra standard, Plystra's participation is stated publicly on the product's website and in its repository.

Plystra does this to learn from people who build well and to let Plystra grow through work it is proud of. It does not charge partners for it.

This document states what a partnership is, how one forms, who owns what, who is responsible for what, how Plystra's participation is shown, and how a partnership ends. It is the basis for written agreements; it is not itself an agreement.

## Current status

`Planned`. No partnership has been signed. The terms below describe the intended arrangement and must be reviewed by a lawyer and an accountant before the first agreement is signed; see [Before the first agreement](#before-the-first-agreement).

## What a partnership is

A partnership is a relationship between Plystra LLC and one author: an individual developer or a studio of a few people. Plystra does not partner with larger companies, does not invest, and does not take equity.

The partnership differs from the relationships defined in [Plystra Craft](https://github.com/plystra/craft#scope-and-project-relationships):

| Relationship | Who owns the product | Plystra's role | Public wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plystra-owned project | Plystra LLC | Owner and steward | A Plystra project. |
| Sponsored project | Its independent owner | Agreed support only | Sponsored by Plystra. |
| **Partner project** | **The author** | **Co-developer and seller** | **An agreed statement of Plystra's participation** |

A partner project is not a Plystra-owned project and must not be described as one. Plystra's work on it is deeper than sponsorship: Plystra takes part in product decisions and in the work itself, and its participation is stated publicly once the product has earned it.

## What each side contributes

Plystra contributes its time and judgment. It works on the product alongside the author, including design, engineering, writing, naming, and release preparation, and it takes part in product decisions throughout the partnership. Everything Plystra contributes belongs to the author; see [Ownership](#ownership). Plystra LLC acts as the seller: it sells the product to customers worldwide, collects payment, and pays the author. Plystra charges no fee, commission, or revenue share.

The author contributes the product and is responsible for it. The author maintains the product, shares its source with Plystra as described in [Source sharing](#source-sharing), and carries every obligation to customers described in [Responsibility for sales](#responsibility-for-sales).

## How a partnership forms

A partnership forms in stages, and either side may stop at any stage before an agreement is signed.

1. **Conversation.** Plystra and the author talk at length about the product, how the author works, and what each side expects. Plystra partners with people before it partners with products; a strong product from someone Plystra does not trust does not proceed.
2. **Trial collaboration.** Plystra and the author work together on one concrete piece, such as a feature or a release, for two to four weeks. The trial shows how each side writes code, handles feedback, keeps commitments, and resolves disagreement.
3. **Written agreement.** Both sides sign an agreement that records the terms in this document, the minimum term, the Craft provisions the product must meet, and anything specific to the product.
4. **Co-development.** Plystra and the author work on the product together. The product uses its own name and makes no public reference to Plystra.
5. **Standard review.** Plystra's steward reviews the product against the agreed standard. If it meets the standard, Plystra's participation may be stated publicly. If it does not, work continues or the partnership ends, and the public never learns of the association.
6. **Release and continuing review.** Plystra LLC sells the product. The standard continues to apply to every release, and Plystra may review the product at any time.

Plystra takes on only as many partnerships as it can work on deeply. A candidate may be asked to wait.

## Ownership

The author owns the product. The copyright in the product's code, design, and content belongs to the author, and the product's domain is registered to the author.

Plystra's contributions belong to the author. All code, designs, writing, and ideas that Plystra contributes to the product during the partnership are the author's, and Plystra assigns its rights in them to the author and keeps no claim to them. This holds whether or not the partnership continues; an author who leaves early owes compensation as described in [Ending a partnership](#ending-a-partnership), but the contributions remain the author's.

Two things are not contributions to the product and are excluded from that assignment. First, materials Plystra created outside the partnership and brings into it, such as Plystra's own libraries, tools, and components, remain Plystra's; the author receives a license to use them in the product. Second, the Plystra name, wordmark, and visual identity remain Plystra's and may be used only as described in [Stating Plystra's participation](#stating-plystras-participation).

## Source sharing

The author shares with Plystra the source of what the author provides: the product itself and its official website. Plystra may read, review, and change all of it for the whole partnership.

Third-party services, tools, libraries, and scripts that the author does not provide are not shared, but each one must be declared in writing: what it is, who provides it, what it does in the product, and what data it receives. The author keeps this declaration current and updates it before any release that adds, removes, or changes a third-party dependency. An undeclared third-party dependency is a breach of the agreement.

Plystra keeps its own copy of the shared source, under its own control, and updates it continuously. Access must not depend on the author's continued permission during the partnership, so that a disagreement cannot remove Plystra's ability to see what is being sold.

The author keeps the domain in good standing for the whole partnership, with automatic renewal and registrar lock enabled.

## Responsibility for sales

Plystra LLC is the seller, but the author bears every obligation that follows from the sale. This includes refunds, chargebacks and disputes, customer support and after-sales service, and all subscriptions, including subscriptions sold before the partnership began or continuing after it ends. The author answers customers, resolves their problems, and bears the cost of doing so.

The author bears all legal liability that arises because the author did not share information with Plystra. This includes undeclared third-party dependencies, undisclosed data collection, known defects or security weaknesses, and any fact about the product, its rights, or its customers that the author knew and did not disclose. If such a liability reaches Plystra LLC, the author indemnifies Plystra LLC for it in full.

Plystra LLC may deduct refunds, chargebacks, and related costs from amounts owed to the author, and may hold back part of the proceeds while claims are open.

When a problem of this kind arises, the author must resolve it promptly. If the author does not resolve it, Plystra ends the partnership immediately, as described below.

## Stating Plystra's participation

Plystra's participation is stated publicly only after the product meets the Plystra standard and Plystra's steward confirms it in writing. Before that, the product carries no Plystra name, mark, or reference anywhere.

Once confirmed, the product's official website and its repository must each state clearly that Plystra participates in the product. The wording is agreed in writing for each product and need not follow a fixed form. For example:

> Made by [Author] with Plystra.

> Developed in partnership with Plystra.

> [Product] is a partnership between [Author] and Plystra.

Whatever the wording, it must make Plystra's participation plain to a reader, and it must not describe the product as a Plystra-owned project or as a Plystra project. The product's own name stays first, and the author is credited by name. Receipts and store listings name Plystra LLC as the seller.

Permission to state Plystra's participation ends when the partnership ends or when Plystra withdraws it.

## Decisions

The author and Plystra make product decisions together. When they disagree about the product itself, such as its features, roadmap, or technical approach, the author decides.

Plystra decides anything that carries the Plystra name. Plystra may withhold or withdraw the statement of participation from any product or release, and it may refuse to sell a release, on grounds of quality, privacy, security, honesty of claims, or fit with the Plystra brand. Plystra does not need the author's agreement to do so.

## Ending a partnership

### When Plystra ends it

Plystra may end a partnership at any time, immediately and without the author's agreement. Grounds include, without limitation:

- an obligation under [Responsibility for sales](#responsibility-for-sales) that the author does not resolve;
- unlawful conduct connected with the product or the author;
- infringement, plagiarism, or a license violation in the product;
- malicious code, undisclosed data collection, an undeclared third-party dependency, or a concealed security weakness;
- a release that falls below the agreed standard and is not corrected;
- public conduct by the author that harms Plystra's reputation;
- any other circumstance in which Plystra judges that continuing would harm the Plystra brand.

### When the author ends it

The author may not leave at will. Every agreement sets a minimum term of at least 12 months from signing. During the minimum term, the author may end the partnership only with Plystra's written consent or if Plystra materially breaches the agreement and does not correct the breach. After the minimum term, the author may end the partnership with 90 days' written notice.

An author who leaves takes the product with Plystra's contributions in it, so leaving early has a cost. If the author ends the partnership before the minimum term ends, other than for Plystra's uncorrected breach, or if Plystra ends it for the author's breach, the author compensates Plystra for its contributions. The agreement fixes how compensation is calculated, based on Plystra's recorded contributions at an agreed rate, and Plystra keeps a record of its contributions throughout the partnership for this purpose.

In every case, the author must settle all obligations under [Responsibility for sales](#responsibility-for-sales) before the partnership is considered ended. Those obligations survive the end of the partnership.

### Separation

When a partnership ends, the separation is complete within 3 days. Plystra LLC stops selling the product. The author removes every statement of Plystra's participation and every Plystra name and mark from the product, its website, its repository, and its listings, and moves or cancels all subscriptions sold through Plystra LLC. The author may not describe the product as formerly made with Plystra or otherwise refer to the past association unless Plystra agrees in writing. The product keeps its own name and continues under the author.

Proceeds owed to the author up to the end date are paid, less refunds, chargebacks, related costs, and any compensation owed to Plystra.

## Acquisition

Plystra does not promise to acquire any partner product. Acquisition is possible, but no conversation and no partnership commits Plystra to it, and Plystra does not suggest otherwise to win a partnership.

The agreement gives Plystra a right of first negotiation. If the author decides to sell the product, the author first negotiates with Plystra in good faith for an agreed period before offering it to anyone else.

## Payments and tax

Plystra LLC is the seller of record, either directly or through a merchant-of-record platform that handles sales tax and VAT. The author receives the proceeds less third-party costs, such as payment processing, platform fees, and taxes that must be withheld by law, and less the deductions described in [Responsibility for sales](#responsibility-for-sales). Every payment to an author follows a written agreement, and every author completes the tax forms United States law requires, such as Form W-8BEN for a foreign individual. Plystra LLC withholds tax where the law requires it. Each author is responsible for tax and currency rules in their own country.

Plystra does not collect or move money informally on anyone's behalf.

## Before the first agreement

The following must be settled with a lawyer and an accountant before the first agreement is signed:

- the form of the agreement, including the assignment of Plystra's contributions, the license of Plystra's existing materials, the license to state Plystra's participation, the minimum term, termination, compensation for early exit, and the right of first negotiation;
- how the author's responsibility for sales is made enforceable while Plystra LLC is the seller of record, including indemnity, deductions, and holdback;
- the author's warranties about originality, licensing, disclosure, and security, and the author's responsibility for breaches of them;
- whether partner products should be sold through Plystra LLC or through a separate entity that isolates their liabilities;
- the choice of merchant-of-record platform, and how refunds, chargebacks, and subscriptions are handled and moved within 3 days at separation;
- tax treatment of payments to authors, including withholding and treaty rates;
- customer data: who controls it during the partnership and what happens to it at the end.
