# Plystra Partnerships

## Purpose

The purpose of a Plystra partnership is to make products together with excellent people. An independent product creator, or a very small studio, and Plystra shape a product together: its direction, the problem it solves, its experience and design, how it is described, and how it is sold. The form of that collaboration is not fixed. It may be conversation and criticism, shared decisions, or hands-on work, in whatever mix suits the product and the people. When the product meets the Plystra standard, Plystra's participation is stated publicly on the product's website and in its repository.

Selling the product is not the purpose of a partnership, and Plystra does not offer partnerships as a distribution or payment service. If the author wants it, Plystra LLC, a United States company, can also put the product on sale and collect payment from customers around the world, either as the seller itself or through a merchant-of-record platform. That is an optional addition to a partnership, never the reason for one.

Plystra does this to learn from people who build well and to let Plystra grow through work it is proud of. It does not charge partners for it, and it does not ask for equity, a share of revenue, or ownership of anything the author makes.

This document states what a partnership is, the two forms it takes, how one forms, who owns what, how the product is sold, what each side promises, how a partnership ends, and how a partner product may become a Plystra project. It is the basis for written agreements; it is not itself an agreement.

## Current status

`Planned`. No partnership has been signed. The terms below describe the intended arrangement and must be reviewed by a lawyer and an accountant before the first agreement is signed; see [Before the first agreement](#before-the-first-agreement).

## The principle

Plystra controls only what is Plystra's: its name, its time, and its storefront. The author controls the product, its customers, and whether to stay.

The two halves depend on each other. Plystra may withdraw its name or stop selling a release at its own discretion, because the Plystra name must stay attached only to work it stands behind. That discretion is fair only because the author can leave freely, at little cost, with the product, its customers, and an honest record of the work. A partnership that gave Plystra broad control and also made leaving expensive would be a trap, and the authors Plystra most wants to work with would rightly refuse it.

Trust is established before the agreement, by choosing people carefully, and not afterwards by penalties.

## What a partnership is

A partnership is a relationship between Plystra LLC and one author: an individual product creator or a studio of a few people. Plystra does not partner with larger companies, does not invest, and does not take equity.

The partnership differs from the relationships defined in [Plystra Craft](https://github.com/plystra/craft#scope-and-project-relationships):

| Relationship | Who owns the product | Plystra's role | Public wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plystra-owned project | Plystra LLC | Owner and steward | A Plystra project. |
| Sponsored project | Its independent owner | Agreed support only | Sponsored by Plystra. |
| **Partner project** | **The author** | **Product partner, and sales channel when agreed** | **An agreed statement of Plystra's participation** |

A partner project is not a Plystra-owned project and must not be described as one. Plystra's part in it is deeper than sponsorship: Plystra thinks the product through with the author and takes part in its decisions, and its participation is stated publicly once the product has earned it.

## Two forms of partnership

A partnership takes one of two forms, and the agreement names which. Every partnership is a product partnership; selling through Plystra LLC is an option the author may add.

**Product partnership.** Plystra thinks the product through with the author, takes part in its decisions, and states its participation once the product meets the standard. The author makes the product's sales arrangements independently, through the author's own channels and under the author's own name or company, and carries every obligation they bring. Who the seller of record is depends on the structure the author chooses; if the author uses a merchant-of-record platform, the platform is the seller. Plystra has no license to sell the product, no sales account for it, and no part in its payments. Nothing is exclusive.

**Product and sales partnership.** Everything in a product partnership, and in addition Plystra LLC sells the product worldwide and collects payment for it. Because customers need one place to buy the product, one set of terms, and one party to ask about payment, the right to sell is exclusive to Plystra LLC for as long as the sales arrangement lasts, as described in [The license to sell](#the-license-to-sell).

The sections on the license to sell, how the product is sold, payments, reserves, and moving customers apply only to a product and sales partnership. Everything else applies to both forms.

The form can change. A product partnership becomes a product and sales partnership by a written amendment both sides sign. A product and sales partnership becomes a product partnership when either side gives 30 days' written notice to end the sales arrangement alone; see [Ending the sales arrangement](#ending-the-sales-arrangement).

## How a partnership forms

A partnership forms in stages, and either side may stop at any stage before an agreement is signed.

1. **Conversation.** Plystra and the author talk at length about the product, how the author works, and what each side expects. Plystra partners with people before it partners with products; a strong product from someone Plystra does not trust does not proceed.
2. **Trial collaboration.** Plystra and the author take on one concrete question about the product together, such as a decision, a design, or a release, for two to four weeks. The trial shows how each side works, makes decisions, uses tools, handles feedback, keeps commitments, and resolves disagreement. It shows the author as much about Plystra as it shows Plystra about the author. A short trial agreement is signed before the trial begins; see [The trial agreement](#the-trial-agreement).
3. **Written agreement.** Both sides sign an agreement that records the terms in this document, the Craft provisions the product must meet, and anything specific to the product.
4. **Working together.** Plystra and the author shape the product together, in whatever form suits it. The product uses its own name and makes no public reference to Plystra.
5. **Standard review.** Plystra's steward reviews the product against the agreed standard. If it meets the standard, Plystra's participation may be stated publicly. If it does not, work continues or the partnership ends, and the public never learns of the association.
6. **Release and continuing review.** The product goes on sale, through the author's own channels or through Plystra LLC, according to the form of the partnership. The standard continues to apply to every release, and Plystra may review the product at any time.

## The trial agreement

The trial happens before the partnership agreement, so it has its own short agreement, signed before any work or source is exchanged. It settles three things.

First, contributions. Everything Plystra contributes during the trial belongs to the author, on the same terms as in [Ownership](#ownership). Plystra's existing materials used in the trial remain Plystra's, and the author receives a perpetual license to use them in the product.

Second, confidentiality. Each side keeps the other's source, unreleased plans, and non-public information confidential, and uses it only to evaluate the partnership. The limits of this obligation are the same as in [What Plystra promises](#what-plystra-promises).

Third, what happens if no partnership follows. Either side may end the trial at any time, for any reason, without owing anything. If the trial ends without a partnership agreement, the author keeps everything produced in the trial, Plystra deletes its copy of the author's source, and neither side mentions the trial publicly unless both agree. The trial creates no obligation to sign a partnership agreement and gives Plystra no right of first negotiation.

## Ownership

The author owns the product. The copyright in the product's code, design, and content belongs to the author, and the product's name and domain belong to the author.

Plystra's contributions belong to the author. All code, designs, writing, and ideas that Plystra contributes to the product during the partnership are the author's. Plystra assigns its rights in them to the author, keeps no claim to them, and asks for nothing in return, whether the partnership lasts or not.

Two things are not contributions to the product and are excluded from that assignment. First, materials Plystra created outside the partnership and brings into it, such as Plystra's own libraries, tools, and components, remain Plystra's; the author receives a perpetual license to use them in the product, which continues after the partnership ends. Second, the Plystra name, wordmark, and visual identity remain Plystra's and may be used only as described in [Stating Plystra's participation](#stating-plystras-participation).

## Source sharing

The author shares with Plystra the source of what the author provides: the product itself and its official website. Plystra may read, review, and change all of it for the whole partnership.

Third-party services, tools, libraries, and scripts that the author does not provide are not shared, but each one must be declared in writing: what it is, who provides it, what it does in the product, and what data it receives. The author keeps this declaration current and updates it before any release that adds, removes, or changes a third-party dependency.

Plystra keeps its own copy of the shared source and updates it continuously, so that a disagreement cannot remove Plystra's ability to see the product that carries its name. The obligations Plystra accepts for that copy are listed in [What Plystra promises](#what-plystra-promises).

The author keeps the domain in good standing for the whole partnership, with automatic renewal and registrar lock enabled.

## The license to sell

In a product and sales partnership, the author grants Plystra LLC a license to distribute and commercialize the product. The license lets Plystra LLC host, reproduce, market, distribute, and sell the product; use the product's name, logo, screenshots, and descriptions to do so; and authorize the payment processor, merchant-of-record platform, or app store in use to complete the transactions, issue receipts, and deliver the product to customers. The license transfers no ownership and gives Plystra no right to change the product's license terms for customers.

For as long as the sales arrangement lasts, the right to sell the product is exclusive to Plystra LLC. Neither the author nor anyone the author authorizes sells the product, or paid access to it, through any other channel, so that customers have one place to buy it, one set of terms, and one party to ask about payment. The exclusivity covers sales only. The author keeps every other right in the product: to develop and modify it, to use it, to publish its source under a license of the author's choosing, and to offer any free edition the agreement names. Exclusivity is consistent with [the principle](#the-principle) only because the author can end it at any time; an author who wants to sell the product elsewhere ends the sales arrangement, and may keep working with Plystra as a product partnership.

The agreement lists any channels through which the author sold the product before the partnership, and states how each is closed or moved. Subscriptions sold there remain the author's responsibility, as described in [What the author promises](#what-the-author-promises).

Exclusivity ends on the end date of the partnership, or of the sales arrangement alone, so that the author may begin selling independently during the sales transition. The license itself continues, without exclusivity, until the sales transition ends, or ends at once when the partnership ends for serious cause, except as needed to honour customers who have already paid. Licenses that customers received with their purchases continue under the product's own terms.

The product's customers are the author's customers, in either form. Customer and subscription records sit in Plystra LLC's sales account on the author's behalf, and are handed to the author when the sales arrangement ends, as described in [Moving customers and subscriptions](#moving-customers-and-subscriptions).

## How the product is sold

This section applies only to a product and sales partnership. In a product partnership, the author makes the product's sales arrangements independently, and the seller of record is whoever the author's chosen structure makes it.

Each agreement names one of two sales structures for the product. The difference matters because the seller of record is the party customers buy from, and it carries the seller's obligations to them.

**Direct sale.** Plystra LLC is the seller of record. It sells to customers through a payment processor such as Stripe, issues receipts in its own name, and carries the seller's obligations under consumer protection and tax law, including registering for and collecting sales tax and VAT where required.

**Platform sale.** A merchant-of-record platform such as Paddle is the seller of record. Customers buy from the platform, which issues receipts, handles sales tax and VAT, and applies its own refund and dispute rules. Plystra LLC is the platform's vendor: it holds the account, lists the product, and receives payouts from the platform.

In either structure, this document calls the account that holds the product's customers, subscriptions, and payouts Plystra LLC's sales account, and calls whichever party is the seller of record the seller. Plystra's role toward the author is the same in both: it runs the sales account, passes the proceeds to the author, and takes no share.

## What Plystra promises

Plystra makes these commitments to every author, and the agreement records them:

- **No charge.** Plystra takes no fee, commission, revenue share, equity, or payment of any kind for its work, during the partnership or after it.
- **No commitment on involvement.** Plystra does not commit to any amount or kind of contribution: no quantity of code, design, writing, ideas, or time. What Plystra contributes follows what the product needs and what Plystra can give at the time, and a period with little or no contribution is not a breach.
- **Confidentiality.** Plystra keeps the author's unreleased source, trade secrets, unreleased plans, and other non-public information confidential, and uses them only for the partnership. Information that is public, that Plystra already knew, or that Plystra develops independently is not confidential.
- **Independent work stays free.** Confidentiality does not stop Plystra from independently developing products in any field, including ones similar to the author's, so long as it does not use the author's confidential information. During the partnership, Plystra tells the author before it begins work on a product that would compete directly with the partner product.
- **Prompt, transparent payment.** In a product and sales partnership, Plystra LLC sends the author a statement every month and pays within 30 days after the month ends. Any amount held back is shown on the statement with the reason.
- **Credit.** Wherever Plystra's participation is stated, the author is named, and the product's own name comes first.
- **Its own mistakes.** In a product and sales partnership, Plystra LLC bears the consequences of its own errors in running the sales account, such as incorrect charges, payout errors, and misconfigured listings, and indemnifies the author for them.
- **A clean end.** When the partnership ends, Plystra stops using the author's source, keeps an archived copy only where the law or an open claim requires it, and deletes it when that need ends.

## What the author promises

The author makes these commitments to Plystra, and the agreement records them:

- **The product is the author's to offer.** Its code and content are original or properly licensed, and its license obligations are met.
- **Nothing is hidden.** Third-party dependencies are declared, data collection is disclosed, and known defects or security weaknesses that matter to customers are disclosed to Plystra.
- **Customers are looked after.** The author provides support and after-sales service, sets the refund policy and decides individual refunds, bears the cost of refunds, and handles chargebacks and disputes about the product. In a product and sales partnership, this covers every subscription sold through Plystra LLC. In a product partnership, the author also carries whatever obligations the author's own sales arrangements bring.
- **Problems are resolved.** When a customer problem or a breach of these promises arises, the author resolves it promptly.

The author bears the consequences of breaking these promises, including legal liability that reaches Plystra LLC because the author did not disclose something, and indemnifies Plystra LLC for it.

In a product and sales partnership, the author's refund decisions are bounded by the obligations of the seller under the sales structure in use: consumer protection law in each customer's jurisdiction, the rules of the payment processor or merchant-of-record platform, including refunds the platform grants on its own authority, and the published terms of sale. Plystra LLC may issue a refund directly through its sales account when one of these requires it, or when a customer's refund request has gone unanswered by the author for 5 business days. The cost of such a refund is the author's unless it results from Plystra LLC's own error.

In the same form, Plystra LLC may deduct refunds and chargebacks from amounts owed to the author, and may hold back a reserve to cover refunds, chargebacks, and disputes that can still arise. Because that risk depends on the payment channel, the product, and its subscription terms, the agreement sets the reserve for each product: its size, how long each amount is held, and when it is released. The reserve is stated before the partnership begins, shown on every statement, and released when its period ends.

## Stating Plystra's participation

Plystra's participation is stated publicly only after the product meets the Plystra standard and Plystra's steward confirms it in writing. Before that, the product carries no Plystra name, mark, or reference anywhere.

Once confirmed, the product's official website and its repository each state clearly that Plystra participates in the product. This statement is a current endorsement, as defined in [Current endorsement and historical attribution](#current-endorsement-and-historical-attribution), and lasts only while Plystra permits it. The wording is agreed in writing for each product and need not follow a fixed form. For example:

> Made by [Author] with Plystra.

> Made in partnership with Plystra.

> [Product] is a partnership between [Author] and Plystra.

Whatever the wording, it must make Plystra's participation plain to a reader, and it must not describe the product as a Plystra-owned project or as a Plystra project. Receipts name the seller under the sales structure in use.

## Decisions

The author and Plystra make product decisions together. When they disagree about the product itself, such as its features, roadmap, pricing, or technical approach, the author decides.

Plystra decides what carries its name and what it sells. Plystra may withdraw the statement of its participation, or, in a product and sales partnership, decline to sell a particular release, on grounds of quality, privacy, security, honesty of claims, or fit with the Plystra brand. It does not need the author's agreement to do so, and it explains its reasons to the author. If the author disagrees, the author is free to leave.

## Ending a partnership

Either side may end a partnership. How it ends depends on why.

### Current endorsement and historical attribution

Two kinds of statement are treated differently when Plystra withdraws its name or a partnership ends.

A **current endorsement** is any statement that Plystra participates in the product now, or any use of the Plystra name, wordmark, or visual identity on a current surface: the product, its official website, its repository's README and description, its store listings, and its current marketing. Current endorsement exists only while Plystra permits it.

A **historical attribution** is a truthful, past-tense statement of what happened, such as "Made with Plystra from 2027 to 2028" or "Versions 1.0 to 1.4 were made with Plystra". It names no Plystra mark beyond the plain name, and it must not suggest that Plystra participates now or endorses the product.

Records that cannot or need not be rewritten are neither. Version control history, commit authorship, changelogs and release notes for past releases, and legally required notices stay as they are in every case, and neither side asks the other to alter them.

### Withdrawing the Plystra name

Plystra may withdraw its current endorsement at any time, at its own discretion, and explains its reasons to the author. The author removes every current endorsement within 3 days. Unless the withdrawal is for serious cause, the author may then keep a historical attribution covering the period before the withdrawal.

Withdrawing the name does not by itself stop sales or end the partnership.

### Ordinary end

The author may end the partnership at any time with 30 days' written notice, for any reason, including to sell the product independently or through a company of the author's own. Plystra may end it with the same notice. No fee, compensation, or payment is owed by either side for ending it.

The partnership ends on the last day of the notice period. On that date, current endorsement ends and is removed within 3 days, Plystra's participation in the product stops, the right of first negotiation ends, and Plystra stops using the author's source.

In a product partnership, nothing further follows: the author already sells the product and keeps doing so.

In a product and sales partnership, a sales transition follows the end date, so that the author can move customers and subscriptions to the author's own sales, as described in [Moving customers and subscriptions](#moving-customers-and-subscriptions). During the transition, only these obligations continue: the product stays on sale through Plystra LLC's sales account without the Plystra name, renewals, refunds, and chargebacks keep being processed, and monthly statements and payments continue; the author continues to provide support and bears the costs described in [What the author promises](#what-the-author-promises); and both sides keep their confidentiality obligations. When the transition ends, the product comes off sale through Plystra LLC, and Plystra LLC pays the final statement once the reserve period has run.

After an ordinary end, the author may keep a historical attribution.

### Ending the sales arrangement

In a product and sales partnership, either side may end the sales arrangement alone with 30 days' written notice, for any reason, and keep working together as a product partnership. On the last day of the notice period, exclusivity ends and the author may sell the product independently. A sales transition then follows exactly as in an ordinary end, and customers and subscriptions move as described in [Moving customers and subscriptions](#moving-customers-and-subscriptions). Current endorsement, Plystra's participation in the product, and the right of first negotiation continue.

### Ending for serious cause

Plystra ends the partnership immediately, without notice and without a sales transition, when any of these occurs:

- malicious code, undisclosed data collection, or a concealed security weakness in the product;
- unlawful conduct connected with the product or the author;
- infringement, plagiarism, or a license violation that the author does not correct;
- an undeclared third-party dependency or another undisclosed fact that exposes customers or Plystra to harm;
- a customer problem or breach of the author's promises that the author does not resolve;
- public conduct by the author that seriously harms Plystra's reputation.

The partnership ends on the day Plystra gives notice. Within 3 days, the author removes every current endorsement and, in a product and sales partnership, new sales and renewals through Plystra LLC's sales account stop. No historical attribution may be made on any current surface.

Ending the relationship at once does not end what paying customers are owed. In a product and sales partnership, customers who have already paid are handled as described in [Moving customers and subscriptions](#moving-customers-and-subscriptions), in whatever time the law and the platforms require, and at the author's cost. Plystra LLC pays the final statement once the reserve period has run, less refunds, chargebacks, and costs for which the author is responsible.

### Moving customers and subscriptions

This section applies whenever a sales arrangement ends, whether with the partnership or alone.

Subscriptions often cannot simply be copied from one account to another. A payment processor may copy customer and payment data between accounts without copying the active subscriptions, which must be recreated. Leaving a merchant-of-record platform involves transferring payment credentials and importing customers, and can take weeks. Moving customers is therefore planned work with steps the platforms control, not a single act either side can complete on demand.

In an ordinary end, or when the sales arrangement alone ends, both sides begin preparing the move when notice is given, and the agreed target is to complete it within 60 days after the end date. If the move is not complete by then because of the platforms' own processes rather than delay by either side, the transition is extended for as long as those processes reasonably require, up to a further 60 days.

Any subscription that still cannot be moved, and every subscription after an end for serious cause, is wound down rather than renewed. The customer is told in advance that the subscription will not renew through Plystra LLC and, where the author continues the product, how to continue with the author. Every period a customer has paid for is either honoured until it ends, with the author keeping the product working for that customer, or refunded pro rata, in either case at the author's cost. No customer is charged for a period they cannot use, and no customer loses a period they have paid for.

Customer and subscription records are handed to the author as far as the law and the platform's terms allow. After an end for serious cause, Plystra LLC may withhold records where handing them over would expose customers to the harm that caused the end.

### What survives

Whatever the reason for the end, these obligations continue after it: confidentiality, the assignment of Plystra's contributions and the license of Plystra's existing materials, each side's indemnity for events before the end, the author's responsibility for refunds and chargebacks on sales made through Plystra LLC, and Plystra LLC's obligation to pay what it owes.

## Becoming a Plystra project

A partner product may become a Plystra-owned project, but only when the author and Plystra both want it. Plystra does not promise this to any author, does not suggest it to win a partnership, and never makes it a condition of anything. An author who prefers to keep the product is never at a disadvantage for that choice.

The change is made by a separate written transfer agreement, negotiated for the particular product. Under it, Plystra LLC acquires the copyright in the product, its name, and its domain, and takes over the customer relationships as far as the law allows. Whether Plystra pays for the transfer, and what the author's role is afterwards, are agreed freely between the two sides. The author may continue as a maintainer under a separate agreement, or step away.

Before the transfer, both sides confirm that the author has the right to transfer every asset named in it. This means checking the licenses of third-party components, obtaining assignments or permissions from anyone else who contributed code, design, or content, and confirming that the name and domain are free of competing claims. Assets the author cannot transfer are named in the agreement and handled separately.

A product can make this change only if it meets the conditions the [Charter](https://github.com/plystra/craft/blob/main/CHARTER.md) sets for every Plystra-owned project, including a commitment to the full Craft philosophy, and only if Plystra can maintain it with the resources it has.

After the transfer, the product follows the rules for Plystra-owned projects and may say "A Plystra project". The author is credited as its original creator permanently, for example "Created by [Author]", on the product's website and in its repository.

The partnership agreement also gives Plystra a right of first negotiation for the duration of the partnership. If the author decides to sell the product, the author first negotiates with Plystra in good faith for 30 days before offering it to anyone else. The right does not prevent the author from leaving, or from running the product through a company of the author's own.

## Payments and tax

In a product partnership, no money passes between Plystra and the author: the author collects the proceeds of the author's own sales, and Plystra charges nothing.

In a product and sales partnership, the following applies. Under a direct sale, Plystra LLC is the seller of record and is responsible for registering for, collecting, and remitting sales tax and VAT where required. Under a platform sale, the platform is responsible for tax on sales, and Plystra LLC receives payouts from it as its vendor. In both, the author receives the proceeds less third-party costs, such as payment processing, platform fees, and taxes that must be withheld by law, and less the deductions described in [What the author promises](#what-the-author-promises). Every payment to an author follows a written agreement, and every author completes the tax forms United States law requires, such as Form W-8BEN for a foreign individual. Plystra LLC withholds tax where the law requires it. Each author is responsible for tax and currency rules in their own country.

Plystra does not collect or move money informally on anyone's behalf.

## Before the first agreement

The following must be settled with a lawyer and an accountant before the first agreement is signed:

- the agreement for each form of partnership, and the amendment and notice used to change from one form to the other;
- the trial agreement, including the assignment of trial contributions, confidentiality, and deletion of source when no partnership follows;
- the form of the partnership agreement, including the license to distribute and commercialize the product and the scope of its exclusivity, the assignment of Plystra's contributions, the perpetual license of Plystra's existing materials, the license to state Plystra's participation, confidentiality and its exception for independent development, termination, and the right of first negotiation;
- the mutual indemnities, and how the author's responsibility for customers is made enforceable under each sales structure;
- the author's warranties about originality, licensing, disclosure, and security;
- whether partner products should be sold through Plystra LLC or through a separate entity that isolates their liabilities;
- the refund policy and Plystra LLC's terms of sale, and the reserve for each product, set from the actual payment channel, chargeback windows, and subscription terms;
- for each sales structure, Plystra LLC's obligations as seller or as platform vendor, and the choice of payment processor or platform;
- confirmation from the chosen payment processor or platform that Plystra LLC may sell products made by third-party authors under its account, and whether a separate arrangement for platforms or marketplaces is required;
- for products sold through app stores, which developer account lists the product and how the listing moves to the author at the end of a partnership;
- how subscriptions and customer records are moved to the author at the end of a partnership under each platform's actual migration process, and how prepaid periods are honoured or refunded;
- the transfer agreement used when a partner product becomes a Plystra project, including how the author's right to transfer each asset is verified;
- tax treatment of payments to authors, including withholding and treaty rates;
- customer data: Plystra LLC's role while it holds the data, and the lawful basis for handing it to the author.
