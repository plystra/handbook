# Plystra Partnerships

## Purpose

A Plystra partnership is a way for an excellent independent developer, or a very small studio, to build a product together with Plystra and release it worldwide. Plystra works on the product itself: design, code, writing, and review. Plystra LLC, a United States company, sells the product and collects payment from customers around the world. When the product meets the Plystra standard, Plystra's participation is stated publicly on the product's website and in its repository.

Plystra does this to learn from people who build well and to let Plystra grow through work it is proud of. It does not charge partners for it, and it does not ask for equity, a share of revenue, or ownership of anything the author makes.

This document states what a partnership is, how one forms, who owns what, what each side promises, and how a partnership ends. It is the basis for written agreements; it is not itself an agreement.

## Current status

`Planned`. No partnership has been signed. The terms below describe the intended arrangement and must be reviewed by a lawyer and an accountant before the first agreement is signed; see [Before the first agreement](#before-the-first-agreement).

## The principle

Plystra controls only what is Plystra's: its name, its time, and its storefront. The author controls the product, its customers, and whether to stay.

The two halves depend on each other. Plystra may withdraw its name or stop selling a release at its own discretion, because the Plystra name must stay attached only to work it stands behind. That discretion is fair only because the author can leave freely, at little cost, with the product, its customers, and an honest record of the work. A partnership that gave Plystra broad control and also made leaving expensive would be a trap, and the authors Plystra most wants to work with would rightly refuse it.

Trust is established before the agreement, by choosing people carefully, and not afterwards by penalties.

## What a partnership is

A partnership is a relationship between Plystra LLC and one author: an individual developer or a studio of a few people. Plystra does not partner with larger companies, does not invest, and does not take equity.

The partnership differs from the relationships defined in [Plystra Craft](https://github.com/plystra/craft#scope-and-project-relationships):

| Relationship | Who owns the product | Plystra's role | Public wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plystra-owned project | Plystra LLC | Owner and steward | A Plystra project. |
| Sponsored project | Its independent owner | Agreed support only | Sponsored by Plystra. |
| **Partner project** | **The author** | **Co-developer and seller** | **An agreed statement of Plystra's participation** |

A partner project is not a Plystra-owned project and must not be described as one. Plystra's work on it is deeper than sponsorship: Plystra takes part in product decisions and in the work itself, and its participation is stated publicly once the product has earned it.

## How a partnership forms

A partnership forms in stages, and either side may stop at any stage before an agreement is signed.

1. **Conversation.** Plystra and the author talk at length about the product, how the author works, and what each side expects. Plystra partners with people before it partners with products; a strong product from someone Plystra does not trust does not proceed.
2. **Trial collaboration.** Plystra and the author work together on one concrete piece, such as a feature or a release, for two to four weeks. The trial shows how each side writes code, handles feedback, keeps commitments, and resolves disagreement. It shows the author as much about Plystra as it shows Plystra about the author.
3. **Written agreement.** Both sides sign an agreement that records the terms in this document, the Craft provisions the product must meet, Plystra's expected involvement, and anything specific to the product.
4. **Co-development.** Plystra and the author work on the product together. The product uses its own name and makes no public reference to Plystra.
5. **Standard review.** Plystra's steward reviews the product against the agreed standard. If it meets the standard, Plystra's participation may be stated publicly. If it does not, work continues or the partnership ends, and the public never learns of the association.
6. **Release and continuing review.** Plystra LLC sells the product. The standard continues to apply to every release, and Plystra may review the product at any time.

Plystra takes on only as many partnerships as it can work on deeply. A candidate may be asked to wait.

## Ownership

The author owns the product. The copyright in the product's code, design, and content belongs to the author, and the product's name and domain belong to the author.

Plystra's contributions belong to the author. All code, designs, writing, and ideas that Plystra contributes to the product during the partnership are the author's. Plystra assigns its rights in them to the author, keeps no claim to them, and asks for nothing in return, whether the partnership lasts or not.

Two things are not contributions to the product and are excluded from that assignment. First, materials Plystra created outside the partnership and brings into it, such as Plystra's own libraries, tools, and components, remain Plystra's; the author receives a perpetual license to use them in the product, which continues after the partnership ends. Second, the Plystra name, wordmark, and visual identity remain Plystra's and may be used only as described in [Stating Plystra's participation](#stating-plystras-participation).

The product's customers are the author's customers. Plystra LLC holds customer and subscription records as the seller, on the author's behalf, and hands them to the author when the partnership ends, as far as the law allows.

## Source sharing

The author shares with Plystra the source of what the author provides: the product itself and its official website. Plystra may read, review, and change all of it for the whole partnership.

Third-party services, tools, libraries, and scripts that the author does not provide are not shared, but each one must be declared in writing: what it is, who provides it, what it does in the product, and what data it receives. The author keeps this declaration current and updates it before any release that adds, removes, or changes a third-party dependency.

Plystra keeps its own copy of the shared source and updates it continuously, so that a disagreement cannot remove Plystra's ability to see what is being sold under its name. The obligations Plystra accepts for that copy are listed in [What Plystra promises](#what-plystra-promises).

The author keeps the domain in good standing for the whole partnership, with automatic renewal and registrar lock enabled.

## What Plystra promises

Plystra makes these commitments to every author, and the agreement records them:

- **No charge.** Plystra takes no fee, commission, revenue share, equity, or payment of any kind for its work, during the partnership or after it.
- **Real involvement.** The agreement states how much time Plystra expects to give the product and how quickly it responds. If Plystra cannot keep to that, it says so.
- **Confidentiality.** Plystra treats the author's source and unreleased plans as confidential and uses them only for the partnership. Plystra does not use them, or the author's product ideas, to build a competing product.
- **Prompt, transparent payment.** Plystra LLC sends the author a statement every month and pays within 30 days after the month ends. Any amount held back is shown on the statement with the reason.
- **Credit.** Wherever Plystra's participation is stated, the author is named, and the product's own name comes first.
- **Its own mistakes.** Plystra LLC bears the consequences of its own errors as the seller, such as failed or incorrect charges, payout errors, and problems with its storefront, and indemnifies the author for them.
- **A clean end.** When the partnership ends, Plystra stops using the author's source, keeps an archived copy only where the law or an open claim requires it, and deletes it when that need ends.

## What the author promises

The author makes these commitments to Plystra, and the agreement records them:

- **The product is the author's to offer.** Its code and content are original or properly licensed, and its license obligations are met.
- **Nothing is hidden.** Third-party dependencies are declared, data collection is disclosed, and known defects or security weaknesses that matter to customers are disclosed to Plystra.
- **Customers are looked after.** The author provides support and after-sales service, decides and bears the cost of refunds, and handles chargebacks and disputes about the product. This covers every subscription sold through Plystra LLC.
- **Problems are resolved.** When a customer problem or a breach of these promises arises, the author resolves it promptly.

The author bears the consequences of breaking these promises, including legal liability that reaches Plystra LLC because the author did not disclose something, and indemnifies Plystra LLC for it.

Plystra LLC may deduct refunds and chargebacks from amounts owed to the author. It may hold back up to 10% of a month's proceeds for up to 120 days to cover open chargebacks and disputes, and releases the remainder when that period ends.

## Stating Plystra's participation

Plystra's participation is stated publicly only after the product meets the Plystra standard and Plystra's steward confirms it in writing. Before that, the product carries no Plystra name, mark, or reference anywhere.

Once confirmed, the product's official website and its repository each state clearly that Plystra participates in the product. The wording is agreed in writing for each product and need not follow a fixed form. For example:

> Made by [Author] with Plystra.

> Developed in partnership with Plystra.

> [Product] is a partnership between [Author] and Plystra.

Whatever the wording, it must make Plystra's participation plain to a reader, and it must not describe the product as a Plystra-owned project or as a Plystra project. Receipts and store listings name Plystra LLC as the seller.

## Decisions

The author and Plystra make product decisions together. When they disagree about the product itself, such as its features, roadmap, pricing, or technical approach, the author decides.

Plystra decides what carries its name and what it sells. Plystra may withdraw the statement of its participation, or decline to sell a particular release, on grounds of quality, privacy, security, honesty of claims, or fit with the Plystra brand. It does not need the author's agreement to do so, and it explains its reasons to the author. If the author disagrees, the author is free to leave.

## Ending a partnership

Either side may end a partnership. How it ends depends on why.

### Withdrawing the Plystra name

Plystra may withdraw the statement of its participation at any time, at its own discretion. When it does, the author removes every Plystra name and mark from the product, its website, its repository, and its listings within 3 days. Withdrawing the name does not by itself stop sales or end the partnership.

### Ordinary end

The author may end the partnership at any time with 30 days' written notice, for any reason, including to sell the product independently or through a company of the author's own. Plystra may end it with the same notice. No fee, compensation, or payment is owed by either side for ending it.

After the notice, Plystra LLC continues to sell the product without its name for a transition period of 60 days, so that the author can move customers and subscriptions to the author's own sales. Plystra LLC then hands over customer and subscription records, stops selling, and pays the final statement.

After an ordinary end, the author may keep an accurate historical statement of the partnership, such as "Started with Plystra" or "Developed with Plystra from 2027 to 2028". It must describe the past relationship truthfully and must not suggest that the partnership continues.

### Ending for serious cause

Plystra ends the partnership immediately, without notice and without a transition period, when any of these occurs:

- malicious code, undisclosed data collection, or a concealed security weakness in the product;
- unlawful conduct connected with the product or the author;
- infringement, plagiarism, or a license violation that the author does not correct;
- an undeclared third-party dependency or another undisclosed fact that exposes customers or Plystra to harm;
- a customer problem or breach of the author's promises that the author does not resolve;
- public conduct by the author that seriously harms Plystra's reputation.

Separation is complete within 3 days. Plystra LLC stops selling the product at once and ends or hands over all subscriptions, and the author removes every Plystra name and mark from the product, its website, its repository, and its listings. The author may not refer to the past association in any form. Plystra LLC hands over customer and subscription records as far as the law allows, and pays the final statement, less refunds, chargebacks, and costs for which the author is responsible.

## Acquisition

Plystra does not promise to acquire any partner product. Acquisition is possible, but no conversation and no partnership commits Plystra to it, and Plystra does not suggest otherwise to win a partnership.

The agreement gives Plystra a right of first negotiation for the duration of the partnership. If the author decides to sell the product, the author first negotiates with Plystra in good faith for 30 days before offering it to anyone else. The right does not prevent the author from leaving, or from running the product through a company of the author's own.

## Payments and tax

Plystra LLC is the seller of record, either directly or through a merchant-of-record platform that handles sales tax and VAT. The author receives the proceeds less third-party costs, such as payment processing, platform fees, and taxes that must be withheld by law, and less the deductions described in [What the author promises](#what-the-author-promises). Every payment to an author follows a written agreement, and every author completes the tax forms United States law requires, such as Form W-8BEN for a foreign individual. Plystra LLC withholds tax where the law requires it. Each author is responsible for tax and currency rules in their own country.

Plystra does not collect or move money informally on anyone's behalf.

## Before the first agreement

The following must be settled with a lawyer and an accountant before the first agreement is signed:

- the form of the agreement, including the assignment of Plystra's contributions, the perpetual license of Plystra's existing materials, the license to state Plystra's participation, confidentiality, termination, and the right of first negotiation;
- the mutual indemnities, and how the author's responsibility for customers is made enforceable while Plystra LLC is the seller of record;
- the author's warranties about originality, licensing, disclosure, and security;
- whether partner products should be sold through Plystra LLC or through a separate entity that isolates their liabilities;
- the choice of merchant-of-record platform, and how subscriptions and customer records are moved to the author at the end of a partnership;
- tax treatment of payments to authors, including withholding and treaty rates;
- customer data: Plystra LLC's role while it holds the data, and the lawful basis for handing it to the author.
